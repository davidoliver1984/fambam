## How the system works

SQS's system `SentTimestamp` and `ApproximateReceiveCount` attributes provide
queue delay and retry evidence without changing the language-neutral request
contract. The worker extracts the request's trace context, creates a consumer
span, records bounded operational dimensions and reports either a completed or
failed counter outcome. PostgreSQL remains the durable source for run, attempt
and observation data.

The benchmark still invokes the provider directly. Its private JSON contains
the detailed count/timing evidence; the repository records only aggregate
results and limitations, never family images or biometric vectors.

## What I learned

### Laravel and software engineering

Transport-native receive attributes can supply operational queue measurements
without expanding a stable application contract. Failure-rate telemetry should
use bounded categories rather than exception messages.

### Python and image analysis

Count-only annotations support exact-count rates and conservative upper/lower
bounds, but they cannot identify which detection is false. A benchmark should
state that epistemic limit explicitly.

### Security, privacy and operations

Operational usefulness does not require tenant identifiers or biometric
values. Provider/configuration identity, counts, durations, dimensions and
bounded outcome categories are enough to operate this worker while keeping
sensitive analysis in its durable tenant-scoped store.
