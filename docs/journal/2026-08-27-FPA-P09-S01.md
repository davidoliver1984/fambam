# FPA-P09-S01 — Representative local face-analysis benchmark evidence

## Session objective

Gather evidence for ADR-0011 without drafting the ADR or implementing face
analysis. Inspect the existing private uploads without identifying people,
define a private local benchmark representation, record coverage and gaps, and
identify the architectural decisions that S02 must settle.

## Existing foundation

- Phase 5 already provides immutable preserved originals, deterministic
  full-resolution canonical assets, source and canonical SHA-256 identities,
  presentation variants, queued processing, retries and tenant-scoped media
  delivery.
- Phase 6 keeps human-confirmed `PhotoPerson` facts authoritative and distinct
  from later machine-derived observations. Machine output may never overwrite
  those facts.
- The Laravel application already owns tenant context, PostgreSQL RLS,
  authorization, persistence, audit and idempotent job orchestration.
- `apps/image-ai` is currently a stateless FastAPI health/telemetry boundary.
  It has no PostgreSQL access, face-analysis provider abstraction, model,
  biometric persistence or production inference endpoint.
- The local stack already supplies PostgreSQL, Redis/SQS-style queues, object
  storage and OpenTelemetry correlation/trace propagation that Phase 9 can
  reuse after ADR-0011 fixes the service contract and trust boundary.

## Private corpus

The private corpus is stored under the repository-local, ignored
`.local/benchmarks/face-analysis/` directory. `.local/` is explicitly ignored
at the repository root. The directory contains private copies with anonymous
`B001`-style identifiers, a private manifest and annotations, local preparation
and checksum-verification scripts, and local contact sheets. None of those
artifacts is tracked.

All 38 candidate source files match the immutable original SHA-256 values held
for the corresponding ready uploads: 38 checked, zero missing and zero
mismatches. No upload or Photo state was changed. Every candidate contains at
least one visible face and is useful either as an independent benchmark scene
or as repeat-input evidence.

To avoid allowing repeated conversions or near-identical scene captures to
overweight provider accuracy, the corpus distinguishes:

- 29 core, visually independent scenes containing approximately 61 visible
  face instances;
- 9 auxiliary repeated/conversion inputs, retained for checksum, format and
  repeat-behaviour investigation; and
- approximately 79 visible face instances across all 38 images.

The 29-image core contains 10 single-face and 19 multi-face scenes, with at
most four visible faces in one image. Category counts overlap:

## Coverage gaps

The existing corpus is weighted towards recent digital adult photographs. It
does not provide enough evidence for cross-age embedding behaviour or difficult
archival material. Additional consented private family images should include:

- confirmed anonymous same-person series at approximately 20, 40 and 60+
  years, ideally for at least two people;
- confirmed child/teen/adult series;
- old scans, including faded, damaged or creased examples and more than one
  scan resolution;
- consented known siblings, parent/child pairs and similarly featured
  relatives, labelled only with anonymous benchmark groups;
- confirmed same-person examples with and without glasses, facial hair and
  substantial hairstyle or appearance changes;
- more children, more older adults, groups larger than four, extreme profiles
  and stronger occlusion.

Random internet faces are not an acceptable substitute for these gaps.

## Provider-level measurements for later benchmark runs

S01 defines measurements but sets no Person-recognition threshold:

- expected versus detected count, count-exact-match rate, missed faces and
  false positives per image and category;
- detection-confidence distribution without converting it into an identity
  decision;
- bounding-box validity, in-frame coverage, relative/absolute face size and
  manually reviewed plausibility;
- landmark availability/plausibility and alignment success;
- embedding success, expected dimension, finite values and normalization;
- cold/warm latency, p50/p95/max latency, throughput, peak memory and failure
  rate;
- provider, model, model checksum, runtime and configuration identity;
- source checksum and dimensions for every run; and
- repeat-run stability for detection count, boxes, landmarks and embeddings,
  while treating repeated inputs as auxiliary rather than independent accuracy
  evidence.

Phase 10, not S01, owns Person-recognition calibration and thresholds.

## Analysis-source evidence for ADR-0011

- The preserved original has maximum source fidelity, but also retains private
  metadata, uses heterogeneous formats, can be large, and leaves orientation
  and colour handling to every provider. ADR-0007 does not permit it as the
  Phase 9 analysis input.
- The canonical is full-resolution rather than a resized presentation asset;
  it applies orientation, normalizes sRGB, strips privacy-sensitive metadata,
  has a stored checksum, uses a constrained browser-presentable format and can
  be regenerated deterministically from the original. ADR-0007 already names
  it as Phase 9's permitted analysis source. This is the strongest current
  evidence for ADR-0011 to confirm operationally.
- Thumbnail/card variants crop or downsize content and can lose small or
  peripheral faces. The display variant is capped and remains a versioned
  presentation concern, so presentation variants are poor authoritative
  inference inputs.
- A dedicated stored analysis variant would add storage, invalidation and
  versioning complexity. It is not justified by current evidence. If a model
  later requires bounded inputs, ADR-0011 can consider deterministic in-memory
  derivation or tiling from the canonical without changing the source identity.

The local object-storage bucket was empty during this evidence pass, although
the 38 source files remain available and checksum-identical to their database
records. Restoring/regenerating app-managed canonical objects is therefore an
operational prerequisite for an end-to-end provider benchmark, but not a
blocker to completing this evidence-only S01.

## Privacy, tenancy and licensing evidence

- Face-analysis results must remain Family-Space scoped under RLS, with no
  cross-Family-Space matching or index.
- Laravel remains the authorization, tenancy and persistence authority; the
  stateless Python service must not access PostgreSQL.
- Machine observations and embeddings are derived data, never Person identity
  and never replacements for human-confirmed `PhotoPerson` facts.
- Reprocessing or provider/model changes must create explicitly versioned
  derived results rather than silently mutating history or human metadata.
- Images, embeddings, landmarks, private paths and per-image annotations must
  not enter source control or ordinary logs.
- InsightFace-compatible local inference is acceptable for current private,
  non-commercial Fambam development and evaluation. Source-code licensing does
  not imply unrestricted rights to pretrained model weights. Before any
  commercial Fambam deployment, or commercial distribution/use of pretrained
  weights, the exact applicable model licence must be re-evaluated and
  commercial terms or permission obtained where required. This is not an S01
  blocker.

## Decisions ADR-0011 must settle

ADR-0011 must make one coherent Phase 9 decision covering:

1. the Fambam-owned provider-neutral detection, landmark/alignment and
   embedding request/result/error contract;
2. the initial provider, model and weight artifacts, including exact licence,
   provenance, version and checksum;
3. the Python inference boundary, caller/service identity and media-delivery
   trust boundary, while preserving the no-PostgreSQL rule;
4. operational confirmation of the canonical as source asset and mandatory
   source-checksum verification;
5. Laravel-owned queue orchestration, timeouts, bounded retries and provider
   failure mapping;
6. tenant-scoped analysis-run and face-observation persistence, including
   boxes, landmarks, confidence and provider metadata;
7. embedding representation, dimensionality, storage, access controls and
   retention;
8. provider/model/runtime/configuration versioning and configuration hashing;
9. idempotency, concurrent duplicate-work prevention, reprocessing and
   append/version semantics;
10. the strict distinction between derived observations and human-confirmed
    Person/`PhotoPerson` facts, with no cross-tenant identity inference;
11. deletion/export/Family Space teardown treatment for derived biometric
    data;
12. benchmark acceptance gates and operational metrics for detection and
    embedding generation, explicitly excluding Phase 10 identity thresholds;
13. provider upgrade, rollback and coexistence of prior versioned results; and
14. the durable licensing record and commercialisation review trigger.
